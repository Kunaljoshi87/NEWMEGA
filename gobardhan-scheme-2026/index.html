01 / The big picture

A national framework for turning waste into value

GOBARdhan—Galvanizing Organic Bio-Agro Resources Dhan—promotes the conversion of biodegradable resources into renewable fuel and organic manure. The 2026 unified CBG scheme brings several forms of support under one national framework.

Approved by the Union Cabinet on 6 August 2026 and formally launched on 1 October 2026, the scheme covers FY 2026–27 through FY 2035–36. It aims to expand domestic compressed biogas production while connecting farmers, project developers, gas buyers, lenders, and local authorities.

For a developer, the opportunity extends beyond plant construction. Feedstock availability, gas quality, offtake arrangements, digestate utilisation, financing, and statutory approvals all affect whether a project can operate reliably.

Important distinction: The ₹23,731 crore figure is the total national scheme outlay—not the funding available to an individual plant. Individual assistance depends on component-specific eligibility, application, sanction, and applicable guidelines.

07 / Compliance map

Approvals that should enter your project schedule early

Applicable statutory permissions vary by location, plant configuration, storage systems and environmental classification. The following is an indicative planning guide, not a substitute for site-specific legal review.

Land title, permitted land use and local permissions

Confirm title or lease, zoning, development permissions and applicable Gram Panchayat/municipal approvals before freezing the site layout.

Pollution-control consent and environmental requirements

For Maharashtra projects, evaluate MPCB Consent to Establish and Consent to Operate requirements. Assess environmental clearance obligations where applicable. Other states follow their relevant State Pollution Control Board and environmental authority procedures.

Factory, building and fire approvals

Evaluate factory/industrial registrations, building-plan approvals and fire-safety permissions based on project scope and jurisdiction.

PESO and high-pressure gas safety

Identify permissions relevant to regulated gas compression, cylinders, pressure equipment or storage systems before installation and commissioning. Cascade cylinder storage is generally governed by the Gas Cylinders Rules, 2016, and static pressure vessels by the SMPV(U) Rules, 2016.

Electrical and hazardous-area compliance

Carry out hazardous-area classification, earthing, electrical inspection and applicable energisation approvals with qualified professionals.

CGD/OMC gas sale, quality and connectivity

Finalise the buyer or network, quality specifications (CBG is generally specified against IS 16087:2016, with a minimum of 90% methane), metering basis, evacuation arrangement and commercial terms before committing to gas handling equipment and pipeline interfaces.

Scheme sanction, monitoring and claims

Follow the current unified portal, prescribed supporting documents, eligible expenditure requirements and the relevant component's sanction, inspection and disbursement rules.

08 / Project viability

Policy support is only one part of project economics

Project developers should test the business case under realistic combinations of feedstock quantity and quality, processing yield, plant uptime, gas sales arrangements, manure offtake, utility demand and financing costs.

Revenue-side questions

What quantity of saleable CBG will the plant deliver? Is there a documented offtake route? How will gas quality, transport, pipeline injection and payment terms affect net realisation?

Cost-side questions

What are delivered feedstock costs, electricity and water requirements, consumables, labour, maintenance, digestate processing and transport costs?

Engineering risks

Feedstock seasonality, variable methane yield, upset recovery, digestion stability, gas upgrading performance and the reliability of utilities.

Financing risks

Timing of sanction and disbursement, working capital, debt servicing, cost escalation and sensitivity to lower-than-planned production.

Organic manure revenue

Organic manure may provide a separate revenue stream. Under the Department of Fertilizers’ Market Development Assistance (MDA) scheme, ₹1,500 per metric tonne was confirmed in March 2026 for eligible FOM, LFOM and PROM. This is a distinct manure-marketing support measure, not a guaranteed additional payment under every 2026 GOBARdhan project. Actual entitlement depends on eligibility, registration, product compliance and applicable implementation rules.

Carbon credits

Projects that avoid methane emissions may become eligible under the offset mechanism of India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme or voluntary markets. Treat this as upside, not base-case revenue, until a methodology and registration route are confirmed for your project.

Pricing basis: The reported ₹2,110/MMBtu is an administered procurement price framework, not automatically the developer's net realised value after all commercial terms, logistics, quality conditions and taxes.

POLICY VERIFICATION · 02 NOVEMBER 2026

Government policy: what is current and what is historical?

2026 unified GOBARdhan: Union Cabinet approval of 6 August 2026 and the Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas launch of 1 October 2026 describe an integrated programme for FY 2026–27 to FY 2035–36, an overall outlay of ₹23,731 crore, and capital assistance of up to ₹2 crore per TPD of installed CBG capacity for eligible greenfield projects. The described CBO trajectory is 3% in FY 2026–27, 4% in FY 2027–28 and 5% from FY 2028–29.

Older MNRE Waste-to-Energy support: the ₹4 crore per 4,800 kg/day and ₹10 crore project ceiling refer to the earlier programme covering FY 2021–22 to FY 2025–26, not an automatic additional 2026 GOBARdhan grant.

Organic manure: the Department of Fertilizers confirmed MDA of ₹1,500 per metric tonne for eligible FOM, LFOM and PROM in March 2026. Treat it as a separately administered support measure and verify applicability for each project.

Historical CBO announcement: the 2023 statement set voluntary blending through FY 2024–25; 1% in FY 2025–26; 3% in FY 2026–27; 4% in FY 2027–28; and 5% from FY 2028–29. Later 2026 official material states the ongoing 3% / 4% / 5% trajectory.

10 / FAQs & references

Questions project developers frequently ask

Does GOBARdhan registration guarantee a subsidy?

No. Registration supports project identification and relevant workflows. Financial assistance requires component-specific eligibility and sanction under the applicable official guidelines.

Is the ₹2 crore/TPD cap available to every CBG plant?

No. The headline cap applies to eligible greenfield capacity; the applicable project type, expenditure ceilings, additional-capacity rules and sanction terms determine entitlement.

Can an existing plant receive assistance?

The government has indicated that eligible brownfield projects expanding installed CBG capacity can participate. Verify the permitted additional-capacity basis and other criteria in the current handbook.

Is ₹2,110/MMBtu the same as the retail CNG price?

No. It refers to the scheme's administered procurement price for CBG producers, subject to the governing commercial framework, rather than the pump price paid by CNG consumers.

Should an applicant use the older SBM GOBARdhan registration portal?

For CBG projects, the current Ministry of Jal Shakti portal directs applicants to the MoPNG CBG registration route. Always follow the latest unified scheme portal and official application instructions.

Which permits are needed for a CBG plant in Maharashtra?

Requirements may include land-use and local permissions, MPCB consents, fire and factory approvals, electrical safety, PESO-related permissions where applicable, and commercial arrangements with the gas buyer. Confirm the exact list with competent authorities for the specific site and technology.
